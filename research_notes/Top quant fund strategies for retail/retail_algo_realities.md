# Retail algo realities and portfolio-level levers for an automated long-only US momentum strategy

Scope: (a) how retail traders / retail quants actually perform and why backtests fail live; (b) portfolio-level levers (diversification, sizing, leverage, tax, cost, behaviour) for an Israeli resident running an automated long-only US large-cap momentum book. Researched 2026-10-07. Note: several primary PDFs (AQR trend paper) could not be machine-read; numbers from them are flagged as gaps rather than quoted from memory.

## 1. How do retail day traders and retail algo/quant traders actually perform?

### Takeaway
The large-sample evidence is uniformly bad for active retail traders: the more they trade, the worse they do, almost none persist profitably, and there is no evidence of learning. The only large public dataset on retail *algorithmic* strategies (Quantopian, 888 algos) found backtest Sharpe ratios almost useless for predicting live results, and the crowd-sourced fund built on them was closed after underperforming.

### Cited Findings
- Barber & Odean, "Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth" (J. Finance 2000): 66,465 households at a US discount broker, 1991-1996. The highest-turnover households earned 11.4%/yr vs 17.9% for the market; the average household earned 16.4% and turned over 75% of its portfolio annually. The authors attribute the excess trading to overconfidence. — [Barber & Odean, UC Berkeley](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/returns/returns.html); [Hedge Fund Alpha summary](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/common-stock-investment-performance-individual-investors/)
- Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean (Taiwan, complete market transaction data, 1995-1999): less than 1% of all day traders can predictably and reliably earn positive abnormal returns after fees. Far fewer than 20% are profitable after costs in a given period. Top-ranked 500 day traders went on to earn 37.9 bps/day after fees; bottom-ranked earned -28.9 bps/day. New traders keep arriving anyway. — [Barber et al., "Do Day Traders Rationally Learn About Their Ability?" / Day Trading Skill paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf); [IDEAS/RePEc (J. Financial Markets 2014)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v18y2014icp1-24.html)
- Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?" (2019/2020): every individual who began day trading Brazilian equity futures (mini-index) 2013-2015. 97% of those who persisted more than 300 days lost money; only 1.1% earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage and 0.5% more than a bank teller's starting salary. No evidence of learning. — [SSRN 3423101](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101); [FGV repository](https://repositorio.fgv.br/items/e87d04fc-4b4c-4a56-ab5a-1c84c1afde1b/full); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/?p=4771)
- Wiecki, Campbell, Lent & Stauth (Quantopian), "All that glitters is not gold" (SSRN 2745220, 2016): 888 algorithms with at least 6 months of out-of-sample data. Backtest Sharpe ratio had almost no predictive power for out-of-sample performance (R² < 0.025). Backtest volatility and maximum drawdown, plus portfolio-construction features such as hedging, did predict out-of-sample results. The more backtests a user ran on a strategy, the larger the gap between backtest and live results, which is direct evidence of overfitting. A non-linear ML classifier on backtest features got R² = 0.17. — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/in-sample-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-888-trading-strategies); [paper PDF mirror](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/208/All%20that%20Glitters%20Is%20Not%20Gold%20-%20Comparing%20Backtest%20and%20Out-of-Sample%20Performance%20on%20a%20Large%20Cohort%20o.pdf)
- Quantopian closed its crowd-sourced market-neutral fund in February 2020 after it underperformed and returned investor money. The platform shut down in November 2020 after about 9 years, and the co-founders joined Robinhood. Commentators connect the failure to the problem in the company's own 2016 paper: backtests did not predict live returns, and black-box submissions with no economic rationale invite overfitting. WorldQuant, a competitor in crowd-sourced alphas, laid off 130 staff and closed five offices the same year. — [Wikipedia: Quantopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantopian); [QuantRocket blog](https://www.quantrocket.com/blog/quantopian-shutting-down/); [Business of Business](https://www.businessofbusiness.com/articles/how-quantopian-died-shut-down-quant-investment-robinhood/); [Bloomberg/BQ](https://www.bloombergquint.com/amp/businessweek/quant-trading-platform-quantopian-closes-down)

### Inferences
- The evidence above is mostly about high-turnover *discretionary* trading or short-horizon algos. A slow, rules-based, long-only monthly momentum book is structurally different. It has lower turnover, relies on a documented premium rather than skill, and is less exposed to overconfidence-driven trading. The applicable lesson is narrower: discipline about trading frequency, and distrust of in-sample Sharpe.
- The Quantopian result implies that the *risk* statistics of a backtest (volatility, drawdown) are more trustworthy than its *return* statistics. Plan position size and drawdown tolerance from the backtest, but haircut the expected return heavily.
- Every additional backtest variant run on the same data makes the best variant's live results look worse relative to its backtest. An "algo lab" that iterates many filters (crowd/volume, rotation, earnings, etc.) is exactly the setting the Quantopian paper measured.

### Gaps
- No large peer-reviewed dataset of *retail long-only systematic factor* investors' live results was found. Most evidence covers day traders or Quantopian's market-neutral algos.
- The exact size of the backtest-to-live Sharpe decay in the Quantopian cohort (average haircut) was not extracted from the full paper.

## 2. Backtest overfitting: how should an individual judge whether a strategy "improvement" is real?

### Takeaway
After accounting for the number of variants tried, most backtest "improvements" are noise. The academic standards are a t-stat above about 3 (Harvey-Liu-Zhu), a Deflated Sharpe Ratio that accounts for the number of trials and non-normality (Bailey & López de Prado), and an expectation that even real published anomalies lose about a quarter to over half of their return out of sample (McLean-Pontiff).

### Cited Findings
- Harvey, Liu & Zhu, "...and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns" (RFS 2016, vol. 29(1), pp. 5-68): catalogued 316 factors from 313 published papers plus working papers, mid-1960s to 2012. They argue the conventional t > 2.0 cutoff is far too low given collective data mining, and recommend t > 3.0 (p < 0.0027) for a new factor. — [SSRN 2513152](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2513152); [NBER w20592](https://nber.org/papers/w20592); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/taming-the-factor-zoo/)
- Counterpoint: Chen (arXiv 2206.15365) argues that "most claimed statistical findings in cross-sectional return predictability are likely true," i.e. false discovery rates are lower than the factor-zoo critique implies. — [arXiv 2206.15365](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.15365)
- Bailey & López de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio" (J. Portfolio Management 40(5): 94-107, 2014). The DSR corrects for two sources of inflated performance: selection bias from multiple testing, and non-normal returns. It is a Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio (the probability that the true SR exceeds a benchmark) with the benchmark raised to reflect the number and dispersion of trials. They also define Minimum Backtest Length (MinBTL), the length a backtest must have to avoid picking a strategy whose in-sample SR is high but whose true SR is zero. — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551); [Bailey et al. "Statistical Overfitting and Backtest Performance" SSRN 2507040](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2507040); [Bailey overfit tools PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/overfit-tools-at.pdf)
- McLean & Pontiff, "Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?" (J. Finance, Feb 2016): across 97 published predictors, returns are 26% lower out of sample (pre-publication) and 58% lower after publication. They read the 26% as an upper bound on data mining and about 32% as decay from investors trading on the published result. Predictors with higher in-sample returns decay more. — [McLean & Pontiff PDF (Ivey)](https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf); [Gwern mirror](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)
- Quantopian evidence: backtest SR R² < 0.025 for live SR, and more backtesting led to a bigger in/out gap (see Q1). — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/)

### Inferences
Practical rules for an individual, derived from the sources above:
- **Count every variant tried.** Log every backtest configuration, filter, and parameter sweep. Without a trial count, you cannot compute a DSR and cannot tell signal from luck.
- **Rough statistical arithmetic.** The SE of an annual Sharpe ratio estimate is about sqrt((1 + SR²/2)/T) per year of data. For a 20-year backtest that is about 0.23. So a *difference* of 0.2-0.3 in SR between two variants of the same strategy is within noise even before any multiple-testing adjustment. A t-stat of 3 on a stand-alone strategy with SR 0.6 needs (3/0.6)² ≈ 25 years of data. For an "improvement" (an incremental return stream) the required evidence is similar or worse, because variants are highly correlated and the incremental SR is small.
- **Prefer improvements that have (i) an ex-ante economic rationale, (ii) independent published evidence outside your own backtest, (iii) robustness across parameter neighbourhoods, sub-periods and markets, and (iv) small effect on turnover.** Treat a variant whose benefit disappears when a parameter moves by ±20% as overfit.
- **Haircut expected returns 30-60%** relative to backtest/published levels (per McLean-Pontiff), and expect momentum's live premium to be closer to the post-publication range than the 1927-1990s range.
- **Reserve a true holdout** (a period or universe never looked at) and paper-trade forward before going live. Forward months are the only data not contaminated by your own search.

### Gaps
- The exact closed-form DSR formula and recommended thresholds were not extracted. The SSRN abstract and R implementation ([quantstrat deflatedSharpe](https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/quantstrat/versions/0.16.7/topics/deflatedSharpe)) are the references.
- Harvey & Liu's later "Backtesting" / "Lucky Factors" haircut tables (e.g. a 50% Sharpe haircut rule of thumb) were not retrieved in this session.

## 3. How much does combining momentum with uncorrelated return streams help? (core-satellite)

### Takeaway
Diversification across low- or negatively-correlated premia is the most reliable portfolio-level lever. Value and momentum are negatively correlated (about -0.5), so a combination is much closer to the efficient frontier than either alone. Trend following (time-series momentum) has a century-long record of positive returns and of performing well in equity crises. An index core plus a momentum satellite captures much of the premium while sharply limiting tracking-error regret, which is the main thing that causes individuals to abandon a strategy.

### Cited Findings
- Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen, "Value and Momentum Everywhere" (J. Finance, June 2013), 8 markets/asset classes: value and momentum are negatively correlated within and across asset classes, averaging -0.49 within asset classes. A simple 50/50 combination is much closer to the efficient frontier than either alone, with materially higher Sharpe ratios. — [Paper PDF (NYU Stern)](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~lpederse/papers/ValMomEverywhere.pdf); [Swedroe, ETF.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-value-momentum-powerful-combo); [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/value-premium/combining-value-and-momentum-across-asset-classes)
- Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen, "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing" (AQR / JPM 2017): a time-series momentum strategy across global equity index, bond, commodity and currency markets back to 1880 was consistently profitable over 110+ years, with low correlation to traditional assets. Since 1985 it was profitable for nearly all individual futures/forward markets. — [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing)
- Managed futures / trend following was one of the few styles to perform well in 2008. Its "crisis alpha" is debated: is it beta timing or market selection? Trend following also has a documented weak-performance risk *after* crises. — [Fidelity Institutional](https://institutional.fidelity.com/institutions/insights/topics/investing-ideas/trend-following-crisis-alpha-does-it-come-from-beta-timing-or-market-selection); [UCC working paper, "Trend Following" (2014)](https://www.ucc.ie/en/media/research/centreforinvestmentresearch/wp/TrendFollowingFeb2014.pdf); [AQR "Understanding Managed Futures"](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/White-Papers/Understanding-Managed-Futures.pdf)
- Behavioural support for blended designs: Morningstar's "Mind the Gap 2025" finds the investor-return gap is smallest (about 0.2 pp) in allocation funds (balanced/target-date with automatic rebalancing) and widest in sector equity funds. — [Morningstar Mind the Gap 2025](https://www.morningstar.com/business/insights/research/mind-the-gap-2025); [Boldin summary](https://www.boldin.com/retirement/why-investors-miss-out-on-fund-returns/)

### Inferences
- A long-only US large-cap momentum book is largely "market beta plus a momentum tilt." Its correlation to the S&P 500 is high (typically 0.85-0.95 for long-only factor portfolios), so the realistic diversification comes from adding *different asset classes or premia*, not more equity-momentum variants:
  - a value or quality sleeve (negatively or weakly correlated with momentum's active return);
  - trend following on asset classes (managed-futures ETFs), which tends to help in prolonged equity bear markets, exactly when long-only momentum is hurt;
  - bonds/gold/cash as low-equity-correlation ballast.
- Standard portfolio math: combining two streams with equal Sharpe S and correlation ρ gives a combined Sharpe of S·sqrt(2/(1+ρ)). For ρ = -0.5 this doubles Sharpe (×2.0). For ρ = 0 it is ×1.41, and for ρ = 0.9 only ×1.03. This is why adding more equity-momentum variants (ρ ≈ 0.9+) barely helps while adding a weakly correlated premium helps a lot.
- Core-satellite (e.g. 50-70% broad index core + 30-50% momentum satellite) trades some expected excess return for much lower tracking error. That matters because tracking-error regret (lagging the S&P for 2-3 years) is the main reason individuals abandon factor strategies.

### Gaps
- Exact net-of-fee return/vol/Sharpe numbers from Hurst-Ooi-Pedersen, and its "performance in the 10 largest 60/40 drawdowns" table, could not be extracted: the PDFs were encrypted/unreadable in this session. Figures often quoted (net of 2/20 fees, roughly 7%/yr at about 10% vol, positive in most of the largest 60/40 drawdowns) are UNVERIFIED here.
- Trend following's poor 2010s and its 2022 strength are well known but were not sourced in this session.
- No rigorous academic study of retail "core-satellite" outcomes was found. The case is from portfolio math plus behavioural-gap evidence.

## 4. Position sizing and leverage: volatility targeting, fractional Kelly, leverage and leveraged ETFs

### Takeaway
Volatility scaling is well supported for the *momentum* factor specifically, because it reduces momentum crashes. As a general market-timing overlay (Moreira-Muir), it largely fails out of sample. Full Kelly is too aggressive with estimated inputs; half Kelly or less gives up a small part of growth for a large cut in drawdown risk. Leveraged daily-reset ETFs lose value relative to their target multiple in volatile, sideways markets, and that loss grows with volatility and holding period.

### Cited Findings
- Moreira & Muir (J. Finance 2017): scaling factor exposure inversely to recent variance raised Sharpe ratios and certainty-equivalent returns across factors. — [Alpha Architect review](https://alphaarchitect.com/does-portfolio-timing-based-on-volatility-signals-outperform-buy-and-hold/) (page returned 403 when fetched); summary via [Erasmus thesis](https://thesis.eur.nl/pub/49941/Visser_443480.pdf)
- Critique: Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang & Yan (JFE 2020, "On the performance of volatility-managed portfolios") tested 103 equity strategies. They found Moreira-Muir's results rely on in-sample (look-ahead) combination weights. Real-time, implementable versions generally earn *lower* Sharpe ratios and certainty-equivalent returns than the unmanaged portfolios, because the underlying spanning regressions are structurally unstable. — [Cederburg et al. PDF (Lehigh)](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf); [SUFE newsletter](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/355606)
- DeMiguel et al., "A Multifactor Perspective on Volatility-Managed Portfolios" (J. Finance 2024): argue that volatility management *does* add value in a multifactor context, which partially rehabilitates the idea. — [LBS Research Online](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/3716)
- Momentum-specific: Daniel & Moskowitz, "Momentum Crashes" (JFE 2016, data 1927-2013). Momentum crashes are partly forecastable: they occur in high-volatility "rebound" states after bear markets. In March-May 2009 the past-loser decile rose 163% while past winners rose only 8%. An implementable dynamic momentum strategy that scales exposure by forecast mean and variance roughly doubles the alpha and Sharpe of static momentum. They build on Barroso & Santa-Clara's constant-volatility momentum. — [Daniel & Moskowitz PDF (Columbia)](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/4607/mom11.pdf); [Alpha Architect, "Avoiding momentum crashes"](https://alphaarchitect.com/avoiding-momentum-crashes/); [Quantpedia, "Three methods to fix momentum crashes"](https://quantpedia.com/three-methods-to-fix-momentum-crashes/)
- Kelly: under Thorp's continuous (GBM) analysis, full-Kelly betting has about a 50% probability of the bankroll halving at some point. Half Kelly cuts that to 12.5% while lowering the long-run growth rate by only 25%. Thorp used Kelly sizing explicitly in blackjack and at Princeton-Newport. — [LuxAlgo Kelly explainer](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/kelly-criterion/); [PMC article on Kelly](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9955835) (secondary sources; Thorp's original papers not fetched)
- Leveraged ETFs: Cheng & Madhavan (2009), "Dynamics of Leveraged and Inverse ETFs." Because of daily rebalancing, returns over longer horizons depend on the leverage ratio, the index's volatility and the holding period. Underperformance relative to the stated multiple grows with volatility and time, and a round-trip in the index leaves the leveraged ETF below its start. — [ETF.com, "Why do leveraged ETFs decay?"](https://www.etf.com/sections/etf-basics/why-do-leveraged-etfs-decay); [NCCU thesis citing Cheng & Madhavan](https://thesis.lib.nccu.edu.tw/thesis/detail/07892914e48801ef34d3f1ab7fc5f21d)

### Inferences
- **Kelly arithmetic (exact under GBM).** Kelly leverage f* = (μ − r)/σ². At fraction c of Kelly, the growth rate is c(2 − c) of the maximum (c = 0.5 gives 75%), and the probability of ever falling to a fraction x of peak wealth is x^(2/c − 1). For x = 0.5: full Kelly 50%, half Kelly 12.5%, quarter Kelly 0.8%. With an equity-like portfolio (excess return 5%, vol 18%), full Kelly ≈ 1.5x leverage. But μ is the most uncertain input, and overestimating it by 2x puts you at 2x Kelly, which has zero expected log growth. With backtest returns haircut by 30-60% (Q2), a realistic Kelly fraction for an individual is at or below 1x (no leverage).
- **For long-only momentum, the useful sizing lever is a crash-aware volatility scale**: reduce gross exposure, e.g. by raising cash, when realized market/momentum volatility is high, particularly after a bear market. This is better supported than generic market vol-timing (Cederburg et al. critique) and can be implemented long-only by moving to cash/T-bills rather than using leverage.
- **Leverage dangers for an automated account:** margin calls force selling at the worst moment, which turns drawdowns into permanent losses. Daily-reset leveraged ETFs held for months add volatility drag. A momentum book already carries crash risk, and leverage magnifies exactly the left tail that Daniel-Moskowitz document. For an autonomous agent, hard caps on gross exposure (e.g. ≤ 1.0x) are a safety requirement, not an optimisation choice.

### Gaps
- Barroso & Santa-Clara's exact Sharpe improvement (often quoted as roughly 0.5 to roughly 1.0) was not verified in this session.
- No source was fetched quantifying LETF decay over specific periods (e.g. TQQQ vs 3× QQQ realized).

## 5. Taxes and costs for an Israeli resident trading US stocks via a foreign broker

### Takeaway
Israel taxes realized real capital gains and dividends at 25%. The 2025 Arrangements Law adds a combined 5% surtax (3% + 2%) on capital income above about ILS 721,560, so the top rate is 30%. The US generally does not tax a non-resident's stock gains, but withholds 25% on dividends under the treaty (credited against Israeli tax). Foreign brokers do not withhold Israeli tax, so you must self-report annually. Directly-held US stocks above $60k expose heirs to US estate tax up to 40%. Irish-domiciled UCITS ETFs avoid that and cut fund-level dividend withholding to 15%. Turnover matters because every realized gain is taxed immediately at 25-30%, and there is no lower long-term rate in Israel.

### Cited Findings
- Israeli residents pay 25% on capital gains on US securities. In the US, a non-resident alien's capital gains from US stock sales are generally not taxed unless present in the US 183+ days in the year. — [Nefesh B'Nefesh: Capital Gains Tax](https://www.nbn.org.il/life-in-israel/finances/taxes/capital-gains-tax/); [IRS: capital gains of nonresident aliens / 183-day rule](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/the-taxation-of-capital-gains-of-nonresident-alien-students-scholars-and-employees-of-foreign-governments)
- US-Israel treaty: portfolio dividends are withheld at 25% (vs the 30% statutory rate), and 12.5% for 10%+ corporate holders. — [TaxesForExpats: US-Israel tax treaty](https://www.taxesforexpats.com/country-guides/israel/us-israel-tax-treaty.html); [Freeman Law: US-Israel Tax Treaty](https://freemanlaw.com/?p=22025)
- Surtax: from 2025, the 3% surtax applies to taxable income above ILS 721,560, and a new section 121B(a1) adds a further 2% on *capital* income (dividends, interest, capital gains) above the same threshold, for a 5% total surtax on that capital income. — [Barnea Jaffa Lande](https://barlaw.co.il/practice_areas/tax/client_updates/high-income-earners-in-israel-prepare-to-pay-an-additional-surtax/); [Goldfarb, 2025 Israeli tax changes](https://www.goldfarb.com/2025-israeli-tax-changes-worth-following/)
- Self-reporting: foreign brokers such as Interactive Brokers do not withhold Israeli tax at source for Israeli residents, so the taxpayer must report and pay. Some Israeli CPAs offer "hybrid" withholding services for IBKR clients. — [Bloomberg Tax / search summary](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report-international/israel-tax-agency-posts-circular-clarifying-capital-loss-offsets-for-taxpayers); [BSH CPA hybrid withholding service](https://www.bshcpa.co.il/שירותי-ניכוי-מס-hybrid/); [Hasolidit forum thread on annual reporting with a foreign broker](https://www.hasolidit.com/kehila/threads/%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A9%D7%AA-%D7%93%D7%95%D7%97-%D7%A9%D7%A0%D7%AA%D7%99-%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%95%D7%97%D7%99-%D7%94%D7%95%D7%9F-%D7%90%D7%A6%D7%9C-%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%A7%D7%A8-%D7%96%D7%A8-ib.20859/) (community source)
- Capital-loss offset rules (Income Tax Ordinance; clarified in ITA Circular 10/2025):
  - Realized capital losses offset capital gains in the same year.
  - Losses on foreign assets offset foreign gains first, then Israeli gains.
  - Losses on securities may also offset dividends/interest from securities, provided the rate on that dividend/interest does not exceed the corporate tax rate.
  - Unused losses carry forward.
  — [Capitax (Alex Shapira & Co.)](https://www.capitax.co.il/content/2/1906); [Bloomberg Tax on Circular 10/2025](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report-international/israel-tax-agency-posts-circular-clarifying-capital-loss-offsets-for-taxpayers)
- US estate tax: a non-resident alien (non-US citizen) Israeli who dies holding US-situs assets, including shares of US corporations and US-domiciled ETFs, is exposed above a $60,000 exemption, with rates reaching 40% above $1M. There is no unlimited marital deduction for a non-citizen spouse. — [Globes: The US estate tax trap for Israeli investors](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-the-us-estate-tax-trap-for-israeli-investors-1001241321); [S. Horowitz & Co.](https://s-horowitz.com/us-estate-tax-your-american-investments-could-cost-your-heirs-40-of-their-value/); [EisnerAmper](https://www.eisneramper.com/insights/blogs/personal-wealth-advisors-blog/step-israel-pw-blog-0819/)
- Irish-domiciled UCITS ETFs: generally treated as non-US-situs for a non-resident alien, so outside the $60k estate-tax threshold. They suffer about 15% US withholding on underlying US dividends via the US-Ireland treaty, and accumulating share classes reinvest dividends without a taxable distribution. — [TaxesForExpats: UCITS ETF withholding/estate tax (2026)](https://www.taxesforexpats.com/articles/investments/ucits-etf-withholding-tax.html); [MyETFJourney: ETF investing in Israel](https://myetfjourney.com/blog/etf-investing-israel)
- Trading costs: Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz, using about $1 trillion of live institutional trades in 19-21 developed markets (1998-2016), found real-world costs less than one-tenth of earlier academic estimates. Value and momentum survive costs and are scalable; short-term reversal is the most cost-constrained; cost-aware execution raises net returns without much style drift. — [Frazzini-Israel-Moskowitz (NBER conf. paper)](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f68262/f68262.pdf); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/surprise-the-size-value-and-momentum-anomalies-survive-after-trading-costs/); [Swedroe: Trading cost truths](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-trading-cost-truths)

### Inferences
- **Tax drag from turnover.** Israel has no long-term vs short-term distinction, so the cost of turnover is *timing*: realized gains are taxed now instead of being deferred. Illustration: a momentum book returning 10%/yr gross with full annual realization at 25% compounds at about 7.5%. A buy-and-hold book taxed once at the end of 20 years compounds at about 8.8%/yr after tax ((1.1^20 − 1)·0.75 + 1, annualized). Deferral is worth about 1.3 pp/yr in this example. Monthly momentum rebalancing with 100-200% annual turnover realizes most gains within a year, so it is close to the full-realization case.
- **Tax-aware levers specific to this setting:**
  1. Harvest losses: sell losers before year-end, which momentum does naturally since losers drop out, to offset realized gains and, under Israeli rules, possibly dividends.
  2. Prefer holding winners past the rebalance when the signal is only marginally weaker (hysteresis/buffer bands), which cuts both trading costs and realized gains.
  3. Keep the core in accumulating Irish UCITS ETFs: 15% vs 25% dividend withholding, no US estate-tax exposure, no annual dividend events.
  4. Watch the ILS 721,560 surtax threshold. Large realizations in one year can push capital income into the 30% bracket, and spreading realizations across tax years may avoid it.
- **Dividend withholding is a small lever for momentum.** US large-cap momentum stocks tend to be low yielders. At about a 1% yield, the difference between 25% and 15% withholding is about 0.1 pp/yr.
- **Estate tax is a large tail risk.** A directly-held US stock portfolio of, say, $500k could face a six-figure US estate-tax bill. Mitigants are UCITS ETFs for the core, appropriate insurance, or structures discussed with an Israeli/US cross-border tax adviser. The directly-held single-stock momentum sleeve cannot be made non-US-situs without a wrapper. (This needs professional confirmation; it is not tax advice.)
- **Commission and spread costs are not the binding constraint** for a large-cap monthly-rebalanced book of modest size, per Frazzini et al. Taxes and behaviour are.

### Gaps
- Exact treatment of the inflationary/FX component for foreign-currency securities (how Israel computes "real" gain on USD-denominated assets, and whether it can be computed in USD) was not sourced in this session. It needs an Israeli tax-adviser or ITA source.
- Whether Israel credits the full 25% US dividend withholding against Israeli dividend tax (expected: yes, up to the Israeli rate) was not confirmed from a primary source.
- Any 2026 changes to the surtax threshold (indexation) or the capital-income surtax were not checked.
- Whether US estate-tax treaty relief exists for Israel: there is no US-Israel estate tax treaty to my knowledge, but this was not verified.

## 6. Realistic long-run expectations for a concentrated large-cap momentum portfolio

### Takeaway
Momentum is one of the most robust premia and survives realistic costs. But its live, post-publication, long-only, large-cap form delivers a modest edge over the market (a few percent a year at best), with crash risk and long stretches of underperformance. MTUM (since April 2013) has beaten the S&P 500 cumulatively, but not by a large margin, and with notable bad years. Plan for market-like returns plus a small, uncertain premium, not backtest CAGRs.

### Cited Findings
- iShares MSCI USA Momentum Factor ETF (MTUM): launched April 16, 2013; expense ratio 0.15%; about $20.6B AUM; about 129 holdings. It selects large/mid caps on 6- and 12-month price momentum, risk-adjusted by 3-year volatility. The average annual return since inception was about 16.1% per StockAnalysis (October 2026 snapshot; earlier snapshots show 14.9-16.0%). - One aggregator reports MTUM's cumulative return since inception at 169.1% vs 132% for the S&P 500 over a matching window. The window date is unclear and this should be treated as indicative only. — [search result summary of StockAnalysis/Robinhood data](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/mtum)
- Momentum has had difficult rotation years. ETF Trends documents a "tricky year" for MTUM's strategy. — [ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/mtum-tricky-year-momentum-factor-strategy/)

### Inferences
- **Realistic planning numbers** (my synthesis, not a sourced forecast): long-only US large-cap momentum ≈ equity return + 0-3 pp/yr gross. Subtract roughly 0.2-0.5 pp for trading, and 1-2 pp for Israeli tax drag from high turnover relative to a deferred index core (see Q5). The *after-tax* edge over a buy-and-hold UCITS S&P 500 core may then be close to zero or slightly negative unless turnover is controlled. This is the single most important strategic conclusion for this user.
- **The behaviour gap (about 1.2 pp/yr) is comparable to the whole expected momentum premium.** An automated agent removes discretionary panic-selling, but only if the *human* does not switch it off during a 2-3 year underperformance. Pre-commitment rules (e.g. "no strategy changes within N months of a drawdown") are a real lever.
- **Concentration.** A 10-30-stock momentum book has meaningful idiosyncratic risk beyond MTUM's 129 names. Expect higher tracking error and dispersion of outcomes than MTUM, with no corresponding increase in expected premium.
- **Ranking of levers by expected after-tax, after-cost impact for this user** (inference): (1) staying invested / not abandoning the strategy (about 1+ pp/yr); (2) tax deferral via lower turnover, buffers and loss harvesting (about 0.5-1.5 pp/yr); (3) diversifying with low-correlation sleeves (Sharpe improvement, drawdown reduction); (4) crash-aware vol scaling for momentum (drawdown reduction); (5) estate-tax structuring (a tail-risk fix, potentially very large); (6) execution cost (small at this size); (7) signal tweaks (smallest and least reliable, per Q2).

### Gaps

- No sourced estimate was found of the long-only, large-cap, net-of-cost US momentum premium post-2000 (e.g. from AQR's long-only momentum indices or Ken French data for the large-cap high-momentum portfolio). This needs a direct data pull.
