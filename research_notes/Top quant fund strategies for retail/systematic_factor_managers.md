# Systematic, Lower-Frequency Quant Managers (AQR, Man AHL, Winton, DFA, Bridgewater, trend CTAs): What They Do, How They Have Done, What a Retail Long-Only Investor Can Use

Research date: 2026-10-07. Sources are mixed: academic papers (mostly gross, pre-cost, long-short), manager or fund data (net of fees unless noted), index providers, ETF data aggregators, and financial press. Each item says which kind it is where that matters.

## 1. What return sources do these managers use, and how do they combine them?

### Takeaway
The common recipe has four parts. (1) Harvest a handful of well-documented, persistent premia: value, momentum/trend, quality/profitability, low risk/defensive, and carry. (2) Apply them across as many markets and asset classes as possible. (3) Combine premia that have low or negative correlation with each other, above all value with momentum. (4) Size positions by risk (volatility targeting, risk parity) rather than by capital. Most of the edge comes from diversification and risk scaling, not from any one signal.

### Cited Findings
**Value + momentum diversification (AQR's core idea)**
- Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen, "Value and Momentum Everywhere" (Journal of Finance 2013): value and momentum premia show up in 8 markets and asset classes, and value and momentum are negatively correlated within and across asset classes. In the paper's Figure 2 (long-short, scaled to 10% volatility, gross of costs, roughly 1972–2011), US stocks have Sharpe ratios of 0.26 for value, 0.45 for momentum and **0.86 for a 50/50 combination**, with a value–momentum correlation of **−0.65**. The global all-asset version has value 0.51, momentum 0.59, **combo 1.28**, correlation **−0.60**. Value–momentum correlations in individual markets ranged from about −0.35 to −0.65. — [Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2013), PDF](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~lpederse/papers/ValMomEverywhere.pdf)
- The paper names a common global factor structure, partly linked to funding-liquidity risk. — [SUFE summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/360210)
- AQR's mutual fund version, the Style Premia Alternative Fund (QSPIX, launched October 2013), was built to give "pure" long-short exposure to four style premia (value, momentum, carry, defensive) across asset classes. — [Yahoo Finance QSPIX](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/QSPIX/performance); [Advisor Perspectives (Dec 2023)](https://api.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2023/12/04/examining-performance-aqrs-premia-alternative-fund) (article blocked, title and snippet only)

**Trend following / time-series momentum (Man AHL, Winton historically, AQR Managed Futures/Helix, the CTAs)**
- Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen, "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing" (AQR / JPM 2017): an equal-weighted blend of 1-, 3- and 12-month time-series momentum across 67 markets in 4 asset classes, back to 1880. It returned **11.2% a year net of estimated costs and fees (simulated)** with **9.7% volatility**. It made money in **8 of the 10 worst 60/40 drawdowns**. A **20% allocation** raised a 60/40's Sharpe ratio from **0.39 to 0.55** and cut the maximum drawdown from **−62.3% to −50.2%**. — [AQR article](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing?aqrPDF=1); [Yale-hosted PDF](https://fairmodel.econ.yale.edu/ec439/hurst.pdf)

**Risk parity (Bridgewater All Weather)**
- Bridgewater introduced alpha–beta separation in 1990 and risk parity (All Weather) in 1996. All Weather balances risk across growth and inflation regimes by levering lower-volatility assets, especially bonds (about 2x bond exposure). — [Navnoor Bawa Substack (secondary)](https://navnoorbawa.substack.com/p/bridgewaters-alpha-beta-framework); [Angel Investors Network (secondary)](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/market-analysis/risk-parity-hedge-fund-strategy-bridgewater-all-weather)
- Pure Alpha is the discretionary-systematic global macro product, run at 12% target volatility with 30–40 simultaneous trades across bonds, FX, equities and commodities. Its claimed annualized return since December 1991 is about 11.4%. — [Navnoor Bawa Substack (secondary, unverified)](https://navnoorbawa.substack.com/p/bridgewaters-pure-alpha-returned)

**Volatility scaling of momentum**
- Barroso & Santa-Clara, "Momentum Has Its Moments" (JFE, April 2015). Scaling momentum exposure by its realized variance over the previous 6 months "virtually eliminates crashes and nearly doubles the Sharpe ratio". The weights ran from 0.13 to 2.00, averaged 0.90, and hit their lows in the early 1930s, 2000–02 and 2008–09. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/avoiding-momentum-strategy-crashes/); [UCP repository](https://ciencia.ucp.pt/en/publications/momentum-has-its-moments/)

**Long side vs. short side, and firm size (important for long-only)**
- Israel & Moskowitz (JFE 2013), 86 years of US data. **Long positions supply almost all of the size premium, about 60% of the value premium and about half of momentum profits.** The value premium shrinks with firm size and is **weak among the largest stocks**. **Momentum shows no reliable relation to size**, so it works in large caps. Little evidence that trading-cost changes or hedge-fund ownership have eroded these premia over time. — [AQR page](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/The-Role-of-Shorting-Firm-Size-and-Time-on-Market-Anomalies); [SUFE summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/356724)

**Dimensional (DFA)**
- DFA runs long-only, low-turnover funds tilted toward small, value and profitability, with patient trading rather than strict index replication. DFA reports that over the **20 years to September 2024, 84% of its US-domiciled funds beat their benchmarks**, against **17%** for the industry, and that 100% of its funds survived versus 46% industry-wide. — [Dimensional, "The Fund Landscape"](https://www.dimensional.com/chmedia/196713/source/download/the-fund-landscape.pdf). Caveat: this is self-reported and measured against benchmarks DFA chose (style or size indexes), not the S&P 500.

### Inferences
- The managers' "secret sauce" is mostly portfolio construction. The 50/50 value+momentum combination roughly doubles the Sharpe ratio of either factor alone, and the gain comes mainly from the −0.6 correlation, not from better signals.
- For a long-only US large-cap investor, Israel & Moskowitz suggest that **momentum keeps most of its long-side premium in large caps, while value is weakest there**. The value side of AQR's recipe transfers worst to the user's universe.

### Gaps
- I could not reach primary AQR, Man or Winton documents describing their current factor weights. Bridgewater's internal return series come only from secondary sources.
- Moreira & Muir (2017) "Volatility-Managed Portfolios" and AQR's carry papers (Koijen et al.) were not fetched and verified in this pass.

## 2. Realized, after-fee returns over 10–20 years versus simple benchmarks

### Takeaway
The live record is far rougher than the backtests. AQR's multi-style alternative fund lost about 37% cumulatively over 2018–2020, then roughly tripled its money. Trend-following indices returned only about 4–5% a year since 2000, versus about 8% for the S&P 500. They delivered big gains in 2008 and 2022, a weak 2010s, and a roughly 20% drawdown in 2024–25. All Weather had its worst year ever in 2022. Over the 2013–2026 ETF era, long-only factor ETFs beat SPY only through momentum.

### Inferences
- Live, net-of-fee trend returns since 2000 (SG Trend about 5.3%) are about **half the simulated 11.2% net** in Hurst et al. Investors should expect T-bill-plus-a-few-percent with positive crisis convexity, not equity-like returns.
- Factor investing's big drawdowns are long (value: 2018–2020; trend: 2011–2019 and 2024–25). They are survivable only with discipline and modest sizing, and they caused large client outflows: AQR's AUM fell by about a third and Winton's by about 80%. Outflows near the trough are the realized behavioural cost.
- The firms that recovered strongest (AQR) kept their process and spread across many uncorrelated sleeves. Winton's 2018 move away from trend came just before trend's best year (2022), though Winton's diversified fund still did well in 2022.

## 3. How much of the academic premium survives publication, costs and fees?

### Takeaway
About a quarter of in-sample anomaly returns looks like data mining, and roughly another third disappears after publication, presumably arbitraged away. High-turnover anomalies mostly die after trading costs. Many published anomalies do not replicate once microcaps are handled properly. The surviving core is value, momentum, investment and profitability; low turnover helps survival. Expect live, net factor premia to be roughly half or less of paper premia.

### Cited Findings
- **McLean & Pontiff (Journal of Finance 2016)**, 97 predictors. Portfolio returns are **26% lower out-of-sample** (upper bound on data mining) and **58% lower post-publication**, which implies **about 32% decay from publication-informed trading**. Decay is bigger for predictors with higher in-sample returns. Returns stay higher in high-idiosyncratic-risk, illiquid stocks, consistent with limits to arbitrage. — [McLean & Pontiff PDF (Ivey)](https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf); [BC news](https://bc.edu/bc-web/schools/carroll-school/news/2017/pontiff-paper.html)
- The decay is gradual: about 25% lower 3 years post-sample and about 40% at 5 years, reaching the roughly 50% average about 10 years out. — [arXiv "Why and how systematic strategies decay"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01380)
- **Novy-Marx & Velikov (RFS 2016)**, "A Taxonomy of Anomalies and Their Trading Costs". Most anomalies with **one-sided monthly turnover below 50%** keep significant net spreads when designed to reduce costs, but **few higher-turnover strategies survive**. The single best cost-reduction tool is a **buy/hold spread**: keep holding a stock until it falls well past the entry threshold. **Size, value and profitability** have the greatest capacity for new capital. — [NBER w20721](https://nber.org/papers/w20721); [Novy-Marx PDF](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)
- **Hou, Xue & Zhang, "Replicating Anomalies" (RFS 2020)**, 452 anomalies, with NYSE breakpoints and value weighting to blunt microcaps. **65% fail |t|≥1.96**, including **96% of trading-friction anomalies**, and **82% fail** the multiple-testing hurdle of t≥2.78. Microcaps are 3.2% of market cap but 60.7% of stocks. Anomalies that do replicate include **value, momentum, investment and profitability**. — [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/replicating-anomalies/); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v33y2020i5p2019-2133..html)
- **Counterpoint: Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen, "Is There a Replication Crisis in Finance?" (Journal of Finance 2023)**: 153 factors across 93 countries, Bayesian multiple-testing framework. **About 82% replicate robustly.** Factors cluster into **13 themes**, most of which belong in the tangency portfolio, and they work out-of-sample internationally. (Pedersen is an AQR principal, a potential conflict.) — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/is-there-a-replication-crisis-in-finance/); [Inquire Europe](https://www.inquire-europe.org/news/in-case-you-missed-it-lasse-h-pedersen-copenhagen-business-school-nyu-principal-at-aqr/)
- Israel & Moskowitz (2013) found little evidence that size, value or momentum returns changed with falling trading costs or rising hedge-fund ownership over time. — [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/The-Role-of-Shorting-Firm-Size-and-Time-on-Market-Anomalies)

### Inferences
- The disagreement between Hou-Xue-Zhang and Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen is mostly about method. Both agree that the big, economically motivated factors (value, momentum, profitability/quality, investment, low risk) are real. The long tail of obscure anomalies is where the fragility sits.
- For an individual: favour signals that are published, robust and low-turnover; use a buy/hold buffer; keep monthly one-sided turnover well under 50%; and assume the realized premium is about 40–60% of the backtest.
- Fees then take a further bite. The UBS smart-beta results suggest that, on average, product design, costs and timing absorbed whatever premium was left over 10 years.

### Gaps
- No direct estimate found of momentum-specific post-publication decay in US large caps since 1993 (Jegadeesh-Titman). The MTUM ETF record (beating SPY by about 1.7 points a year over 2013–2026) is one live data point, not a study.

## 4. What is realistically usable by a retail investor running a long-only US large-cap (momentum-heavy) strategy?

### Takeaway
The most transferable ideas are these. (a) Pair momentum with something negatively or lowly correlated: value or quality within equities, and, more powerfully, a managed-futures/trend ETF outside equities. (b) Volatility-scale or crash-protect the momentum sleeve. (c) Keep turnover low, with buy/hold buffers and disciplined rebalancing. (d) Size diversifiers at about 10–20% and hold them through multi-year droughts. Leverage, shorting and multi-asset carry, which make up much of AQR's and Bridgewater's edge, are mostly unavailable to a long-only individual, except through ETFs.

### Cited Findings
- **Momentum works in large caps, long-only.** Momentum profits have no reliable relation to size, and about half come from the long side. Value is weak among the largest stocks. — [Israel & Moskowitz 2013](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/The-Role-of-Shorting-Firm-Size-and-Time-on-Market-Anomalies)
- **Value as a momentum hedge.** The US value–momentum correlation is about −0.65, and a 50/50 combination roughly doubles the Sharpe ratio (long-short, gross). — [Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen 2013](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~lpederse/papers/ValMomEverywhere.pdf). Live long-only evidence of the complementarity: in 2023, MTUM +9.15% vs VLUE +14.26%; in 2020, MTUM +29.86% vs VLUE −0.23%; both lagged in some years. — [totalrealreturns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/MTUM,QUAL,USMV,VLUE,SPY)
- **Volatility scaling of momentum** virtually eliminates crashes and nearly doubles the Sharpe ratio (long-short, gross). — [Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015 via CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/avoiding-momentum-strategy-crashes/)
- **Trend-following ETFs as a diversifier.** A 20% trend allocation lifted a 60/40's Sharpe from 0.39 to 0.55 and cut its max drawdown from −62% to −50% (simulated 1880–2016). — [Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing?aqrPDF=1). In 2022 DBMF made +21.6% while SPY lost −18.2%, and MTUM lost −18.3%. — [totalrealreturns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/DBMF,SPY)
- **But the diversifier costs a lot in bull markets.** DBMF compounded at 10.0% vs SPY 16.1% (2019–2026). KMLM returned only 2.25% a year from March 2022 to October 2026, with a −31% max drawdown, worse than SPY's −22% over that window. Three straight negative years (2023–25) for KMLM show that the choice of managed-futures fund matters a lot (2024: CTA +24.2% vs KMLM −1.7%). — [totalrealreturns KMLM/CTA/RPAR](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/KMLM,CTA,RPAR,SPY)
- **Trend can draw down when equities rally.** SG Trend −20.4% vs ACWI +18.3% (May 2024–May 2025), so it is not a reliable hedge on every horizon. — [Cambridge Associates](https://www.cambridgeassociates.com/insight/does-trend-followings-recent-struggle-signal-that-the-strategy-is-structurally-broken/)
- **Risk parity via ETF (RPAR) was a poor diversifier in a rate shock.** −22.8% in 2022, alongside All Weather's −22%, and about 0% annualized from March 2022 to October 2026. — [totalrealreturns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/KMLM,CTA,RPAR,SPY); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/04/23/lackluster-returns-ray-dalio-investing-strategy-investors-pull-billions/)
- **Multi-factor / smart-beta ETFs.** The majority fail to beat their benchmark over 10 years (UBS), and US multi-factor funds lagged by about 1.9%. — [Wealth Professional](https://www.wealthprofessional.ca/investments/etfs/most-smart-beta-etfs-performing-worse-than-the-market/226500)
- **Rebalancing and turnover discipline.** Buy/hold spreads are the most effective cost reducer, and keeping one-sided monthly turnover below 50% keeps anomalies alive after costs. — [Novy-Marx & Velikov](https://nber.org/papers/w20721)
- **Trend managers differ widely even within an index.** Average pairwise correlation was 0.78, but 2024 returns spanned about 15 points. — [SG CTA update 2025](https://content.sgmarkets.com/CTA_UPDATE_KEEPING_UP_WITH_THE_TRENDFOLLOWERS_2025); AQR's Helix made +18.6% vs SG Trend +2.5% in 2025. — [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/aqrs-quant-funds-deliver-strong-returns-amid-volatility-in-2025/)

### Inferences
- For a momentum-heavy long-only US large-cap book, the evidence ranks the additions as follows:
  1. **Volatility scaling or crash protection on the momentum sleeve itself.** Low cost, and the strongest academic support for reducing momentum crashes such as 2009 and the 2022–23 lag.
  2. **A 10–20% managed-futures allocation**, ideally split across two different replicators or managers (for example DBMF plus CTA) to cut manager-specific risk. It pays off in inflationary or rate-shock bear markets like 2022, when stocks, bonds and equity momentum all fell, but costs several points a year in strong equity bull runs.
  3. **A quality or value sleeve within equities** to offset momentum's reversals. Value is the stronger hedge on paper but weaker in large caps.
- Risk parity and multi-factor ETFs have weaker live cases for this investor. RPAR's bond leverage failed in 2022, and multi-factor ETFs have mostly lagged after fees.
- The backtest-to-live haircut (about 50% for anomalies; about 11% simulated vs about 5% live for trend) should be built into any algo-lab backtest. Expect the user's backtested momentum alpha to shrink after publication-era decay, costs and execution.
- The main behavioural risk is abandoning a diversifier during a long drought: trend 2011–2019 and 2023–2025, value 2018–2020. AQR's and Winton's outflows show that even institutions do this near the bottom.

### Gaps
- I found no study that directly measures the marginal benefit of adding a managed-futures ETF to a long-only US large-cap momentum portfolio, as opposed to a 60/40. The correlation between SG Trend and long-only US momentum (MTUM) was not found. Calculating it from DBMF/MTUM monthly returns would be a useful in-house test.
- No live evidence on volatility-scaled long-only momentum ETFs. The Barroso & Santa-Clara results are long-short and gross.
- Fees and tax drag of managed-futures ETFs (K-1-free structures, distributions) were not researched.
